Why Teach Kids Entrepreneurship?

It is often mistaken for career preparation. That is not the aim here.

Teaching children entrepreneurship is often mistaken for career preparation, as if the goal were to produce future founders. That is not the aim here. Most children in this programme will not start a company by sixteen, and that is not how success is measured.

The Core Tenet: Entrepreneurship is taught because the habits behind it are useful to any child, whatever they go on to do. It is a vehicle for personal agency, not an end in itself.

The 5 Lifelong Habits We Build

Click any card to inspect how this habit is cultivated on the physical workshop floor

5 Interactive Habit Cards
Habit 01 👀

Noticing a Problem to Act On

Treating a problem as something to act on, not something that just happens to you.

In The Workshop: Children complete tactile 'Bother Boards'—writing down small daily irritations (like tangled skipping ropes or soggy bookbags) and deciding how to intervene.
+ Expand Workshop Action
Habit 02 📐

Turning Interest into Concrete Plans

Turning a vague interest into a concrete plan, however small.

Why It Matters Later: The exact same skill shows up later in school science projects, personal goals, and creative careers. Dreams become blueprints on graph paper.
+ Expand Workshop Action
Habit 03 ⚖️

Living with Real Constraints

Making choices under limited coins, time, or materials, and living with trade-offs instead of being shielded.

In The Workshop: Every child receives exactly 10 heavy metal play coins. If they spend 6 on ribbon, they cannot afford cardboard. They experience real trade-offs safely.
+ Expand Workshop Action
Habit 04 🗣️

Speaking an Idea Out Loud

Speaking an idea out loud and hearing what other people think of it. Most schooling never asks a child to do this.

In The Workshop: The 'Soapbox Circle'—no PowerPoint or projector screens. Children stand holding their cardboard prototype and present in 90 seconds to peers.
+ Expand Workshop Action
Habit 05 🪙

Understanding Money as a Tool with Trade-Offs

Understanding money early as a practical tool with clear trade-offs, rather than a taboo subject or a source of anxiety.

In The Workshop: Money is demystified into three physical jars: Spend, Save, and Give. Children see money as fuel that directs attention and builds character.
+ Expand Workshop Action
[ SECTION 3 // PEDAGOGICAL CODE ]

The 5 Guiding Beliefs

The non-negotiable principles guiding every facilitator in our physical workshops.

✎ “Facilitators guide the process; they do not supply the answers.”
GUIDING BELIEF 3.1 🎨

Start from the child, not the subject

“Business concepts are introduced only after they are anchored in something the child already feels: a hobby, a person they care about, something that bothers them.”

How This is Structured: This is why the programme opens with self and interest, through the Vision Wheel and the future-self drawing, before it opens with money or ideas. We never ask a child to build a business around an abstraction; we ask them what they love making on Saturday morning.
GUIDING BELIEF 3.2 🪙

Character comes before scale

“Money does not create character in a child. It reveals and amplifies whatever is already there.”

Gentle Early Realisation: A child generous with ten coins will likely be generous with a hundred; a child anxious with ten will likely stay anxious with a hundred. The programme's money activities surface this early and gently, while the stakes are still play money, so children notice their own instincts before the stakes are real.
GUIDING BELIEF 3.3 🍰

Wants are not the enemy

“The programme teaches the difference between wants and needs, but avoids making wants feel shameful.”

Healthy Awareness Without Guilt: Business exists to serve wants as much as needs, and an idea that helps someone get something they want is a legitimate idea. The goal is awareness of the distinction, not guilt about desire.
GUIDING BELIEF 3.4 🧭

Ownership over correctness

“Wherever a child is asked to choose—whether a category, a coin placement, an idea, or a shop name—the choice is theirs, and there is no wrong answer.”

Self-Generated Agency: Facilitators guide the process; they do not supply answers. A confident, self-generated idea is worth more at this age than a technically sound one handed down by an adult.
GUIDING BELIEF 3.5 ✂️

Hands-on over written

“Every concept in the programme has a physical or visual form: cards to sort, coins to place, pictures to draw.”

Reasoning by Doing: Primary-age children reason more reliably through doing than through worksheets or lecture. We keep the desks messy with wood scraps, markers, cardboard, and coins.
